Does Osteopathy Help With Pelvic Girdle Pain?

What pelvic girdle pain is, how osteopathy can help, and the simple exercises and everyday changes that make a difference during pregnancy.

Pelvic girdle pain (PGP) is common in pregnancy — it’s thought to affect around 1 in 5 pregnant women. It can make walking, climbing stairs and even turning over in bed uncomfortable. The good news is that it usually responds well to the right care, and osteopathy can be part of that.

What is pelvic girdle pain?

PGP is pain in the joints of the pelvis — at the front (the pubic symphysis), at the back (the sacroiliac joints), or both. During pregnancy, hormonal changes and the growing weight of your bump change how these joints move and how much load they carry, which can lead to pain and stiffness.

You might feel it over the pubic bone, in the lower back or buttocks, in the groin, or down the inner thighs. It’s often worse with activities that load one leg at a time.

How osteopathy can help

Osteopathy is a hands-on approach that aims to restore comfortable movement and function in the pelvis, lower back and hips. Treatment is always adapted for pregnancy — we use side-lying and seated positions with plenty of support, and gentle techniques suited to your stage.

Does it work?

Research into manual therapy for pregnancy-related pelvic pain is still growing, but studies — including a 2021 observational study of pregnant women receiving osteopathic treatment — have reported improvements in pain and quality of life, particularly over several visits. Many of our patients find a combination of treatment, exercise and practical advice makes daily life much easier.

What to expect at your appointment

We’ll ask about your pregnancy, your symptoms and what makes them better or worse, then gently assess how your pelvis, back and hips are moving. Treatment usually includes hands-on work, some simple exercises and advice for home and work. Most people need a few sessions, and we’ll review how you’re doing each time.

Exercises that can help

Gentle exercise can support the pelvis and keep you moving. Always check with your osteopath or midwife which exercises are right for you, and stop anything that increases your pain.

Everyday tips for pelvic girdle pain

Try to

Keep your knees together when getting in and out of the car or turning in bed · sleep with a pillow between your knees · take stairs one step at a time · rest when you need to · use ice or heat for comfort.

Try to avoid

Standing on one leg (e.g. to put on trousers — sit down instead) · heavy lifting · carrying a toddler on one hip · wide-legged movements such as breaststroke kick.
